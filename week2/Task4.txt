1. og:image (Open Graph Protocol)
<meta property="og:image" content="https://example.com/thumbnail.png">
This tells social media platforms (like WhatsApp, Twitter, or Discord) exactly which image to display as a preview when you share the link.
Without it, platforms might pick a random icon or no image at all, making your link look less professional.

2. viewport-fit=cover (Notch Optimization)
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1.0, viewport-fit=cover">
This is specifically for modern smartphones with "notches" (like the iPhone's Dynamic Island).
It tells the browser to use the full area of the screen, ensuring your website's background extends behind the notch rather than leaving awkward white bars on the sides.

3. color-scheme (Native Theme Support)
<meta name="color-scheme" content="light dark">
It signals to the browser that your site supports both Light and Dark modes.
This allows the browser to automatically render "native" elements—like scrollbars and form buttons—in the correct theme immediately, preventing that bright "flash" of white before your custom CSS loads.
